# Competitive landscape research — August 2026

> **Question:** Is the operator value surface plan servicing a real
> need, or are we building something operators already have? Should we
> proceed?

## The landscape (three tiers)

### Tier 1: Marketplaces (BookRetreats, Retreat Guru, BookYogaRetreats)

These are the incumbents. They own discovery.

- **BookRetreats** — the world's largest retreat marketplace. 20,000+
  hosts, millions of visitors, 100+ countries. Free to list. 14-15%
  commission, with visibility tiers up to 30%. Operators get: listing
  management, booking dashboard, payment collection, inquiry inbox,
  calendar/availability management, early-bird/flash-sale promotions,
  reviews, and a "Host Resource Center" with marketing guides and tools.
  Operators cannot export their guest list or email attendees directly.
  No custom branding. No payment plan automation. No BNPL.
  ([BookRetreats help center](https://help.bookretreats.com/en/collections/116987-hosts),
  [SquadTrip comparison](https://www.squadtrip.com/guides/best-retreat-booking-platforms-compared/),
  [Retreator commission analysis](https://retreator.com/blogpost-how-much-do-retreat-booking-platforms-charge-hosts-in-2026))

- **Retreat Guru** — marketplace + standalone software ($49-149/mo).
  250K+ registrations yearly. Deeper on yoga/meditation lineage and
  teacher detail. Software includes: real-time room inventory, custom
  registration forms with conditional questions, automated pre-arrival
  and post-departure emails, meal reports, housekeeping reports,
  installment payments, waitlists, abandoned-cart lead capture,
  participant portal, API + webhooks, accounting integrations.
  Marketplace commission is 14%. Software is designed for retreat
  centers with fixed locations, not traveling organizers.
  ([Retreat Guru software](https://software.retreat.guru/),
  [Retreat Guru features](https://software.retreat.guru/features),
  [Retreat Guru pricing](https://software.retreat.guru/pricing))

- **BookYogaRetreats** (Tripaneer network) — 8,100+ retreats, search
  by destination/category/date/yoga style. Large directory, thinner
  operator tooling. Same marketplace model.
  ([BookYogaRetreats](https://www.bookyogaretreats.com/))

### Tier 2: Booking software (SquadTrip, WeTravel, Bookinglayer, Gather)

These are the "own your booking" platforms — no marketplace discovery,
but full control over checkout, branding, payments, and guest data.

- **SquadTrip** — the current leader for self-hosted retreat bookings.
  Free tier (Stripe processing ~2.9%), paid plans from $29/mo. Features:
  branded booking pages, automated payment plans, BNPL (Klarna/Affirm/
  Afterpay), guest dashboard, group chat, registration forms,
  e-signatures, room/package management, real-time itineraries, auto-
  payment reminders. Organizers keep ~97% of revenue.
  ([SquadTrip](https://www.squadtrip.com/),
  [SquadTrip vs WeTravel](https://www.squadtrip.com/guides/squadtrip-vs-wetravel-best-retreat-software/))

- **WeTravel** — payment processing + itinerary + booking management.
  Free tier with 3.5% platform fee, paid plans $29-99/mo with lower
  fees. Strong for multi-supplier payouts, weaker on guest management
  and communication.
  ([WeTravel](https://www.wetravel.com/))

- **Gather** — retreat-focused platform with branded website, checkout,
  AI concierge, paid media. £79/mo + 2% per booking. Positioned as
  the "outgrown the marketplace" option.
  ([Gather](https://www.try-gather.co/blog/retreat-guru-alternatives))

- **Bookinglayer** — $69-299/mo. Strong scheduling/accommodation.
  No BNPL, no group chat. Heavier setup.

- **Everybooking, Retreator** — newer entrants competing on flat/zero
  commission. Retreator charges 10% flat. Everybooking charges 0% on
  marketplace bookings.
  ([Everybooking](https://everybooking.com/compare/retreat-guru),
  [Retreator](https://retreator.com/blogpost-how-much-do-retreat-booking-platforms-charge-hosts-in-2026))

### Tier 3: AI travel tools (emerging, not yet retreat-specific)

- **WanderWell** — agentic AI wellness trip planner. Handles flights,
  hotels, and wellness activities. Syncs wearables. India-focused
  inventory. Not a booking platform for operators.
  ([WanderWell](https://wanderwelltravel.lovable.app/))

- **Layla.ai** — AI trip planner with itineraries, flights, hotels.
  General travel, not retreat-specific.
  ([Layla.ai](https://layla.ai/blog/destinations/wellness-travel-destinations-2026))

- **Greytt.ai** — preference-driven itinerary builder. 10 preference
  categories, AI-generated day-by-day plans. Not a booking platform.
  ([Greytt.ai](https://greytt.ai/blog/travel-after-50-purpose-driven-trips))

- **Mindtrip** — AI itinerary platform. General travel.

- **Expedia Trip Matching** — creates itineraries from Instagram Reels.
  General travel, not retreat-specific.

**None of these are retreat-operator platforms.** They are consumer-
facing itinerary tools that don't address the operator's problem of
filling retreats.

## The operator's real problem (confirmed by research)

The research confirms the operator pain points I identified:

1. **Filling retreats is the #1 problem.** Multiple sources (Wanderlust
   Entrepreneur, Retreat Leaders Podcast, RetreatAndGrowRich,
   Retreator) confirm: "Most retreats don't fail because the program is
   poor. They fail because nobody hears about them in time."
   ([Retreator](https://retreator.com/blogpost-how-to-promote-your-retreat-and-actually-fill-it))

2. **Marketing starts too late.** Operators need 3-6 months lead time
   for international guests. Most start 2-3 months out and panic.
   ([Northern Edge Algonquin](https://northernedgealgonquin.ca/2026/03/how-to-market-your-retreat-12-practical-steps-to-fill-your-spaces/),
   [Retreat Leaders Podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/ep-274-why-your-retreat-isnt-filling-even-though-youre/id1186563181?i=1000772194678))

3. **Marketplace commission is a tax, not a service.** Operators pay
   14-30% of total booking value for discovery they can't control.
   Higher tiers buy visibility, not better matches. The commission
   applies to the full booking including cash paid on arrival.
   ([Retreator](https://retreator.com/blogpost-how-much-do-retreat-booking-platforms-charge-hosts-in-2026),
   [Gather](https://www.try-gather.co/blog/retreat-guru-alternatives))

4. **Operators don't own the guest relationship.** BookRetreats doesn't
   let operators export guest lists or email attendees directly. The
   marketplace owns the customer.
   ([SquadTrip comparison](https://www.squadtrip.com/guides/best-retreat-booking-platforms-compared/))

5. **No platform shows demand before conversion.** Every existing
   platform is reactive: the operator lists, waits for inquiries, and
   responds. None proactively surface "here are people whose needs
   match your retreat." The abandoned-cart capture in Retreat Guru is the
   closest, but it only captures people who already started registering
   on that platform — not people articulating intentions elsewhere.

## What no one does (the gap)

After reviewing the full competitive landscape, here is what no
existing platform offers:

### 1. Demand visibility before inquiry

No platform shows operators "3 people are articulating intentions that
match your retreat right now." Every platform waits for the inquiry.
The operator's only signal is page views (if the platform offers
analytics) and inquiries (which are already mid-funnel). The operator
has zero visibility into pre-inquiry demand.

This is the gap the Ardum plan addresses. An intention-first
practitioner flow creates demand signal that doesn't exist on any other
platform. If Mira matches a practitioner to a retreat, the operator can
see that match — before the practitioner inquires, before they hold,
before they book.

### 2. Non-binding holds visible to the operator

BookRetreats and Retreat Guru take deposits. SquadTrip and WeTravel
take deposits. Every platform requires payment to reserve. No platform
has a non-binding, non-charging hold that signals intent without
financial commitment.

Ardum's hold is genuinely different. But currently it's invisible to
the operator. The plan's Phase 3 (hold visibility) makes it visible —
and that visibility is something no competitor offers.

### 3. Practitioner context at booking

When a booking lands on any platform, the operator gets: name, dates,
payment status. Maybe dietary preferences if the platform collects
them. No platform tells the operator "this person was making space for
recovery from burnout, and here's the preparation plan that was
generated for them."

Ardum's preparation plan + intention shape is a relationship surface
that doesn't exist elsewhere. The plan's Phase 4 makes it visible to
the operator.

## Assessment: should we proceed?

**Yes, with a sharpened thesis.**

The competitive research confirms three things:

1. **The operator problem is real.** Filling retreats is the #1 pain.
   Marketing starts too late. Commission is a tax. The guest
   relationship is owned by the platform, not the operator.

2. **No competitor addresses demand visibility.** Every platform is
   reactive (wait for inquiry) or transactional (pay for visibility).
   None proactively surfaces demand before conversion. This is a real,
   unaddressed gap.

3. **The booking management space is crowded and mature.** Retreat
   Guru, SquadTrip, WeTravel, Bookinglayer, Gather — they all do
   calendar, payments, forms, room management, emails. Ardum should
   not compete here. The value is not "another booking platform." It
   is "the only platform that shows you demand before it converts."

### What to adjust in the plan

The research sharpens the plan in three ways:

**1. Drop any ambition to be a booking management platform.**
Phase 1-4 of the plan are correctly scoped — they build demand
visibility, not booking management. Keep it that way. Do not add
calendar, room management, payment plans, or guest CRM. Those are
solved problems with mature competitors. Ardum's wedge is demand
visibility, not operational software.

**2. The pitch to operators is not "list here."**
BookRetreats has 20,000+ hosts and millions of visitors. We cannot
compete on marketplace discovery. The pitch is: "Mira finds
practitioners whose intentions match your retreat and shows you who
they are — before they inquire. You still use SquadTrip or your own
site for the booking. Ardum is the demand layer, not the booking
layer."

This means Ardum should integrate with (or at least link to) the
operator's existing booking platform, not replace it. The /attest flow
publishes a retreat into Mira's matching pool. When a practitioner
holds or books, the operator sees it on /operator. But the actual
booking, payment, and guest management happen wherever the operator
already works.

**3. The commission model should reflect the "demand layer" position.**
If Ardum is not the booking platform, it doesn't charge booking
commission. It charges for demand visibility — a subscription or
per-match fee. This is a different business model from every
competitor, and it aligns with the value: you pay when Mira finds
someone who fits, not when a transaction closes.

### What the plan gets right

- **Operator identity + retreat list** (Phase 1) — minimal, correct.
  Operators need a home base. Don't build a dashboard; build a list.
- **Demand surface** (Phase 2) — this is the wedge. No competitor does
  this. The anonymized intention shapes are the right privacy contract.
- **Hold visibility** (Phase 3) — the non-binding hold is Ardum's
  unique primitive. Making it visible to operators is the thing that
  makes it valuable to them.
- **Preparation context** (Phase 4) — the relationship surface.
  Differentiates from transactional platforms. Not required for the
  first demo but deepens the pitch.

### What to watch for

- **Cold-start problem.** The demand surface only works if there are
  practitioners with active intentions. With zero users, the operator
  sees an empty page. The first operator demo needs seeded practitioner
  episodes to show the surface working. This is a demo problem, not a
  product problem — but it's real.
- **Privacy review.** Showing operators anonymized intention shapes
  is more than any platform shares. The constraints are explicitly
  chosen structured options (not free-text), which keeps it within the
  ADR 0010 privacy contract. But this should be reviewed before
  shipping.
- **Integration story.** If Ardum is the demand layer, not the booking
  layer, the operator needs a way to connect their booking platform.
  For the first version, a simple "booking URL" field on the retreat
  attestation is enough — when a practitioner is ready to book, Mira
  links to the operator's existing checkout. This is a thin integration,
  not a platform integration.

## Conclusion

Proceed. The competitive landscape confirms the gap is real and
unaddressed. The plan is correctly scoped to build the wedge (demand
visibility) without competing in the crowded booking management space.
The pitch to operators is: "Mira finds people who fit your retreat
before they inquire. You see who they are. You keep your booking
platform. Ardum is the demand layer."
